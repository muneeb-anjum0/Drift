# Phase III-E retrieval redesign: preregistration

Status: development-only plan, written before R6 experiments. Phase III-D is a closed, previously used holdout. Its case-level data and traces are not design inputs.

## Task and boundary

Given one change message and the immutable requirements of its authorized project baseline, retrieval returns zero or more requirements that the message may materially affect. Retrieval does not decide the downstream six-class drift label. All baseline requirements with non-empty title or description are candidates; filtering by project and snapshot version happens before scoring. Ranking, thresholding, and the selection cap happen before a selected requirement's description (or title fallback) is sent to the fixed model. A false candidate costs model work and may create a false exposure; a missed candidate cannot be recovered by the classifier.

The operational corpus may contain 0, 1, 2, or 3+ targets and projects of different sizes. No vector service or additional 7B model call is justified at this scale without measured evidence.

## Frozen comparisons and usable data

R0 is the V0 scorer at commit `d07fbb1544a9f190b314d264bb9c9a787b27fbe3`; R5 is the current production scorer, with source hash `04565fc32884031355c796a752c0b1691ff0dc4eb8c894f76d7ecb6e813b89ec`. The Phase III-C development corpora (`retrieval_dev_v1`, `retrieval_dev_v2`) may guide design, as may new explicitly synthetic Phase III-E development cases. Historical regression tests can detect breakage, but are not tuning targets. Phase III-B final, Phase III-D independent final, old held-out raw/model sets, and any new Phase III-E independent final are CLOSED; their case-level content must not guide R6. Adapter-training overlap with any evaluation corpus is UNKNOWN.

R0/R5 normalize to lowercase alphanumeric tokens, remove stop words, normalize simple plurals; R5 additionally strips limited `-ing`/`-ed` suffixes and expands 13 aliases. The lexical score is `min(1, .55*description_overlap + .25*title_overlap + .35*domain_overlap + bonus)`, where each overlap is shared normalized tokens divided by the smaller token-set cardinality, and the bonus is `.12` when at least two direct or title terms match and the pre-bonus score is below `.45`. The specific-match gate requires at least two direct terms or title overlap of `.5`; the score threshold is `.25`; ties keep baseline order; at most three pass. Candidate generation is all scoped, non-empty baseline requirements, not an index search. These frozen implementations are controls, not targets for edits.

## Hypotheses and isolated candidates

- **R6-L1 (BM25):** length-normalized term frequency and inverse document frequency will rank shared-but-specific terms above generic overlap, especially as project size grows; it may still miss low-overlap paraphrases. Use the existing R5 token normalization and no aliases beyond R5, `k1=1.2`, `b=.75`, and title/description as separate fields. Selection calibration is a separate experiment, not bundled with ranking.
- **R6-Q1 (clause decomposition):** taking the maximum lexical score over punctuation/conjunction-delimited user clauses may recover multiple independently stated targets, but could increase false exposures. Keep the underlying R5 scorer, threshold, and cap fixed in this experiment.
- **R6-S1 (small CPU semantic encoder), only if justified by development evidence and resource/licensing audit:** semantic vectors may recover low-overlap positives; however, numbers, negation, and competing requirements may degrade. Compare against the same data and controls before any hybrid test. No GPU or classifier-based query rewriting.
- **R6-H1 (hybrid), only if both component families justify it:** a coarse fixed weight grid, not fine-grained benchmark fitting, may combine semantic recall with lexical specificity. Do not implement or freeze if evidence does not support the additional dependency and latency.

Every experiment uses the same development corpus for R0, R5 and the candidate. Hold ranking constant when studying threshold or cap; hold threshold/cap constant when studying ranking. Record top-1/top-3/top-k reach and precision, MRR, all-target/at-least-one, false exposures, hard-negative contamination, selected and candidate counts, size/category splits and retrieval latency. Trace every candidate score and rejection stage. Rank failures as candidate-generation, ranking, threshold, or cap failures. Selection considers recall, precision, false exposures, size/category robustness, latency, and complexity—not raw recall alone.

R6-L1 is first evaluated with its initial `.25` normalized-score threshold. If ranking improves but this scale rejects most positives, a distinct **R6-L1-cal** selection-only experiment will inspect the coarse thresholds `.05`, `.10`, `.15`, `.20`, `.25` on identical rankings with cap three. This grid is fixed before inspecting threshold results; no cap change is part of it.

For R6-S1, use the 384-dimensional Apache-2.0 `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` encoder at immutable revision `c9745ed1d9f207416be6d2e6f8de32d1f16199bf`. Encode the whole query and each `title + ". " + description` with masked mean pooling and L2 normalization; cosine is the ranking score. No prompt rewrite, GPU, or 7B model call. The coarse selection threshold grid is `.25`, `.35`, `.45`, `.55`, `.65`, with cap three and stable baseline-order ties. Evaluate rank quality before selection tuning. Keep the downloaded weights outside Git. Source/model details: [official model card](https://huggingface.co/sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2), [pinned revision](https://huggingface.co/sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2/tree/c9745ed1d9f207416be6d2e6f8de32d1f16199bf).

R6-S1 development evidence shows a high-recall/high-exposure tradeoff: `.25` gives 36/40 links with 46 false exposures; `.45` gives 30/40 with seven false exposures. R6-H1 therefore tests whether the complementary R5 lexical score can reduce exposure while retaining semantic reach. The **predeclared** coarse grid is lexical weight `alpha ∈ {.25,.50,.75}` and final threshold `{.25,.35,.45}` with cap three, no R5 specific gate. Scores are `alpha*R5_score + (1-alpha)*cosine`; negative cosine is floored at zero. No decimal-place optimization after the grid.

Because whole-message semantic vectors can dilute multi-intent requests, **R6-Q2** tests a single change to R6-S1: split only on commas, semicolons and the standalone word `and`; retain the whole message; discard clauses shorter than three words; score each requirement by the maximum cosine over the whole message and eligible clauses. Keep the same requirement representation, model, cap and threshold grid `.35`, `.45`, `.55`. This is a transparent string split, not generated intent rewriting. Compare multi-target gain against zero-target exposure before selection.

The existing 90% overall target-link recall and 85% per-size retrieval acceptance criteria remain unchanged. A candidate that improves recall by exposing substantially more unrelated requirements is not automatically preferred. The independent generalisation rule and evaluation corpus must be frozen before final predictions; a positive claim requires meeting the unchanged acceptance gates and no material false-exposure or size/category regression. An inconclusive result is distinct from a negative result when valid independent labels or a valid blind run are unavailable.

## Independent-review boundary

After development selection, freeze R6 source, configuration, dependencies, development-data hashes, and results. Then draft a novel small/medium/large final corpus. A reviewer independent of R6 design must adjudicate proposed targets, preserving proposed and reviewed IDs and CONFIRMED/REVISED/AMBIGUOUS/EXCLUDE status. Freeze and hash the reviewed set before running R0/R5/R6 once. If review is unavailable, stop at the review boundary: no self-certified labels, no final evaluation, no V3 or retraining claim.
